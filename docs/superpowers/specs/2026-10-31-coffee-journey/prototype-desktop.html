The journey of

Guji Hambela Natural

From a forest in southern Ethiopia to the cup in your hand: about nine months, and dozens of hands along the way.

This coffeeHow it's usually done

↓ Scroll, or press ↓

1 of 9 · Grown

High in the forests of Guji

Hambela Wamena1,950–2,200 mHeirloom

Smallholder families grow it in the shade of tall forest trees. Nights up here are cool, so the cherries ripen slowly and build up more sugar.

2 of 9 · Picked

Only the ripe ones, by hand

Hand-pickedSeveral passes

Cherries on one branch ripen at different times, so pickers come back again and again for the deep red ones. It takes roughly 6 kg of cherries to make 1 kg of roasted coffee.

3 of 9 · Processed

Dried whole, inside the fruit

Natural process3–4 weeks in the sun

The whole cherry dries on raised beds, turned by hand every day. The bean soaks up the fruit's sugars, and that's where the blueberry and strawberry jam in your cup come from.

4 of 9 · Milled

Out of its shell

Hulled at a dry mill

Once dry, the hard husk and a papery skin called parchment are cracked off. Inside are two green seeds facing each other. They smell like grass and peas, and none of the coffee flavour exists yet.

5 of 9 · Sorted & bagged

Picked over, bean by bean

Ethiopia · Guji60 kg jute sacks

Beans are graded by size and weight. Broken or faulty ones are picked out, often by hand. The best go into jute sacks stamped with where they came from.

6 of 9 · Shipped

Weeks at sea

Via Djibouti4–8 weeks

Green coffee keeps well, so it travels slowly by container ship. A sealed liner inside the sack keeps out moisture until it reaches the roaster.

7 of 9 · Roasted

Where it starts tasting like coffee

Light roastAbout 10 minutes

Heat turns the grassy bean golden, then brown, as its sugars caramelise. At around 200 °C it pops: that's the first crack. Your roaster stopped soon after, so the fruit from the drying stays bright.

8 of 9 · Rested

Letting it breathe

Best on day 10–21

Fresh beans are full of gas, which makes espresso spurt and taste sharp. The little valve lets it out. After a week or two the flavours settle, and this coffee is at its best between day 10 and day 21.

9 of 9 · In your cup

And now, it's yours

18 g → 40 g94 °C

Nine months and two continents later, it ends with 30 seconds in your Meraki. Take a sip: blueberry first, then jam as it cools.
